ECG placement

Correct electrode placement comes before any reading. Misplaced electrodes can create false findings or hide real ones. These are the standard positions for a resting 12-lead ECG.

Before you attach the electrodes: no metal touching the patient. Remove jewellery, watches and other metal objects, and make sure the patient's skin is not touching metal such as bed rails. Move mobile phones and other electrical devices away where possible. Metal contact and nearby equipment can cause electrical interference that blurs the tracing.

Find the landmarks

  1. Feel for the angle of Louis, the ridge where the manubrium meets the body of the sternum. The 2nd rib joins the sternum here.
  2. Slide your finger just below the 2nd rib into the 2nd intercostal space, then count down to the 4th intercostal space.
  3. Place V1 at the right sternal border and V2 at the left sternal border in the 4th space.
  4. Count down one more space on the left and place V4 in the 5th space at the midclavicular line.
  5. Place V3 midway between V2 and V4.
  6. Place V5 (anterior axillary line) and V6 (mid-axillary line) on the same horizontal level as V4.

Colour codes vary by region and manufacturer. Always go by the letters printed on the lead wires. AHA mnemonic for the limb leads: white on right, smoke (black) over fire (red), snow (white) over grass (green).

Getting it right

  • Skin preparation: clean, dry skin; clip hair if needed and gently abrade to lower impedance and artefact.
  • Women and larger breasts: place V3–V6 under the breast, not on top of breast tissue.
  • Limb electrodes on the torso (as used for bedside monitoring) change the ECG. For a diagnostic 12-lead keep them on the limbs, and write on the tracing if torso placement was used.
  • Patient position: lying supine and relaxed, limbs supported, to reduce muscle tremor.
  • Consistency: use the same positions for serial ECGs so changes are real, not due to moved electrodes.

Extra leads when asked

  • Right-sided (V4R): 5th ICS, right midclavicular line, a mirror of V4. Used to look for right ventricular infarction in inferior STEMI.
  • Posterior (V7–V9): same horizontal level as V6, at the left posterior axillary line (V7), the tip of the scapula (V8) and next to the spine (V9). Used when ST depression in V1–V3 suggests posterior MI.
